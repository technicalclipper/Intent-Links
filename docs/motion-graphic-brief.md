# IntentLink — 25s motion graphic brief

For a motion designer. No product knowledge assumed.

---

## What the product does, in one line

You do a task once in your browser. It becomes a link. Someone else opens that
link and **their** AI agent does the same task for them — but only inside the
limits you wrote in plain English.

## The feeling to land

Not "look at our software." Closer to: *I showed you once, and now it just
happens for you — and it can't go past the line I drew.*

Confident, fast, precise. An instrument, not a toy.

## Format

- **25 seconds**, 16:9, plus a 9:16 crop that survives (keep action centred)
- No voiceover — on-screen text only, so it works muted on a feed
- Loopable: the last frame should sit comfortably next to the first

---

## Shot list

### 1 · The problem (0:00 – 0:03)

A long instruction document scrolls past too fast to read. Numbered steps,
screenshots, arrows. It keeps scrolling. It does not end.

> **TEXT:** "Step 1 of 14…"

**Feeling:** tedium. Three seconds is enough — do not linger.

---

### 2 · Do it once (0:03 – 0:08)

Hard cut. A clean, abstract browser window. A cursor moves with purpose and
clicks four times. Each click leaves a **glowing node** behind, and a thin
line connects each node to the next — a chain forming as the cursor works.

Do not show a real product UI. Suggest it: a header bar, a sidebar, soft
rectangles. The nodes are the subject, not the app.

> **TEXT:** "Do it once."

**Feeling:** effortless. The cursor never hesitates.

---

### 3 · Draw the line (0:08 – 0:12)

The chain of nodes slides to one side. A cursor types a sentence, letter by
letter, in plain human handwriting-adjacent type:

> *"only change the theme — don't delete anything"*

As the sentence finishes, it **crystallises**: the words snap into two rigid,
monospaced rule chips that lock into place beside the chain with a
mechanical click.

```
ALLOW   set_theme
DENY    delete_*
```

This is the most important three seconds in the film. The transformation from
soft human sentence to hard machine rule is the whole product.

> **TEXT:** "Say what it must never do."

---

### 4 · It becomes a link (0:12 – 0:15)

The node chain and the rule chips pull together and compress into a single
glowing capsule. The capsule resolves into a short URL.

Then it **launches** — a fast, flat, confident move out of frame. One clean
streak, not a cartoon rocket with flames. Think: a message sent, at speed.

> **TEXT:** "Send the intent, not the instructions."

---

### 5 · It arrives (0:15 – 0:18)

The streak lands in a second frame — clearly a different person's screen.
Keep this unmistakable: a different accent colour on their window chrome, or
split the frame briefly to show both.

A card appears showing what the link will do — three short lines — and one
button: **Run**.

A cursor presses it.

> **TEXT:** "They open it."

---

### 6 · Their agent does the work (0:18 – 0:23)

The nodes from shot 2 reappear in **their** window and light up one by one,
fast — each with a small green tick. Three of them, in about two seconds.
This should feel satisfying and quick.

Then the fourth node reaches for something and **stops dead.**

A red boundary line snaps across the frame. The node recoils. Everything else
holds still.

> **TEXT (large, held):** "Blocked."
> **TEXT (small, beneath, in the creator's handwriting-adjacent type):**
> *"don't delete anything"*

Hold this for a full second. It is the payoff: the rule the person typed in
shot 3 is what stopped it, quoted back in their own words.

---

### 7 · Close (0:23 – 0:25)

Everything clears. Logotype, centred.

> **INTENTLINK**
> *Don't send instructions. Send the intent.*

---

## Visual direction

**Dark.** Near-black ground (#0d0f13), not pure black. Surfaces one step
lighter (#14171d). Hairline borders, 1px, never thick.

**Exactly three signal colours**, and nothing else coloured:

| | | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| green | `#2dd4a7` | allowed, done |
| amber | `#f5b544` | needs a human |
| red | `#ff5c6c` | blocked |

The restraint is the point. When red appears in shot 6 it should be the first
red in the film.

**Two typefaces, used to mean different things:**

- **Monospace** (IBM Plex Mono or similar) = machine truth. Rule chips, the
  URL, verb names.
- **Sans / humanist** (IBM Plex Sans or similar) = human intent. The typed
  sentence, the on-screen captions.

That split is not decoration — it is the product's actual idea, that a person
writes a sentence and a machine enforces it.

**Motion:** precise easing, no bounce, no squash. Things snap into position
like instrument readouts. One exception: the launch in shot 4 may be fast and
loose.

---

## Things to avoid

- Cartoon rocket with fire. The launch is a streak, not a spaceship.
- Robot or brain imagery. There is no mascot.
- Purple-to-blue gradients, floating 3D glass shapes, generic "AI" sparkles.
- Showing a real Chrome window or a real website — stay abstract.
- More than three colours.
- Filling the silence with extra motion. Shot 6's pause is the film.

---

## If 25 seconds is too tight

Cut **shot 1** entirely and open on "Do it once." The problem is implied by
the solution, and the remaining beats each gain a second.

Do **not** cut or shorten shot 6.

---

## One-line summary for an AI video tool

> Dark, precise motion graphics. A cursor performs four clicks that leave a
> chain of glowing nodes. A typed sentence crystallises into monospaced rule
> chips. Nodes and rules compress into a link that launches across to another
> person's screen. They press Run; the nodes light up green one by one, then
> one hits a red boundary and stops. Three colours only — green, amber, red —
> on near-black. No characters, no robots, no gradients.
